# Geometry Reader: The Story

## Where it began
Wasema Farzana grew up in northern Bangladesh, far from the capital, loving mathematics. Wasema attended the best institutions available in the region's town, and still found that geometry was taught as something to memorise rather than something to see. Theorems arrived as dense black-and-white text, with few diagrams that moved and little connection to the physical world. Even with the best the region offered, the resources needed to truly understand the subject were not there.

## What tutoring in Dhaka revealed
Later, Wasema worked as a tutor in Dhaka's most privileged learning environment, where three advantages meet: **affluent neighbourhoods**, **English-medium schools** that follow the Cambridge and Edexcel O- and A-Level curriculum, and an **upper-class social world** that sits around both. There, Wasema saw the other side of the gap, and found it deeper than expected. In these families, spending on a child's education stayed high even when the child struggled with a topic again and again: more tutors, more materials, more support, for as long as it took. A student in the periphery facing the same difficulty would usually have none of this.

The difference was not only money. It came in four layers:

1. **Availability:** whether good resources exist nearby at all.
2. **Affordability:** whether a family can pay for them, and keep paying.
3. **Know-how:** whether a student knows how to learn with those resources.
4. **Networks:** whether a family knows the system, and the people inside it.

Together, these layers shape what is later called "merit". Students who fall behind for lack of access are judged as lacking ability. Geometry Reader starts from the opposite assumption: ability is spread far more evenly than opportunity.

## The principle: understanding, regardless of curriculum
Wasema teaches across Bangladesh's three school systems: the national curriculum in Bangla, its English Version counterpart, and English-medium O-Levels. The syllabuses differ, but the geometry does not. Every student, whichever system they are in, deserves a deep understanding of the topic itself, not just of how one exam asks about it. Geometry Reader is therefore built around **topics first**; matching each lab to a particular syllabus is a separate layer added on top.

## What Geometry Reader does
Geometry Reader is a browser-based geometry sandbox covering Grade 6 to O-Level. Instead of reading a theorem, students drag points, lines and solids and watch the relationships hold:

1. **Direct manipulation.** Every angle, length, area and volume is shown live as the student moves it. Points snap to landmark values such as 90° or 60°, so students *find* the special cases instead of being told them.
2. **Paired vocabulary.** Every formal term sits beside a plain-language version, e.g. *Supplementary Angles ➔ (180° Flat Runway Pairs)*, so the word and its meaning are learned together.
3. **Real-world anchors.** Each lab links its idea to familiar objects, such as scissors, railway crossings, honeycomb cells and bicycle spokes, as a bridge from the abstract to the everyday.

Geometry Reader cannot replace a family's networks or a tutor's attention. It aims to narrow two layers of the gap: putting a high-quality visual resource within reach of any student with a browser (availability), and building the habits of learning from it (know-how).

## Why it starts where it does
The first version is built in English, using the O-Level syllabus, with the students Wasema teaches every day: English-medium and English Version learners. This is deliberate. It is the setting where the design can be tested, observed and refined most closely. Once it works as intended, it will be extended to the students it was conceived for.

## How it is made
Wasema conceived Geometry Reader and directs its design: the curriculum scope, the learners it serves, the interaction principles, the language, and the judgement on every change. The code is written by an AI coding assistant working from Wasema's briefs. See [AI_USE.md](AI_USE.md).

## Where it is going
Geometry Reader is the first of a planned series of visual "Readers" for school and college mathematics.

- **Now:** an English, visual-first version on the O-Level syllabus, refined with English-medium and English Version students.
- **Next:** map every lab to Bangladesh's national **SSC and HSC (NCTB)** syllabus and release a full **Bangla** version, reaching students in the periphery. Then extend to **college-level** topics.
- **Then:** test on low-cost phones and in classrooms outside Dhaka, measuring whether Geometry Reader reduces maths anxiety and deepens understanding. Until then, these aims remain hypotheses.

Geometry Reader grew out of a lifelong love of mathematics and a conviction that where a student lives should not decide how well they can learn.
