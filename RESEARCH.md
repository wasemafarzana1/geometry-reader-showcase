# Research Agenda

Geometry Reader is a design intervention and a research instrument. This document sets out what it is meant to test, how, and under what ethical safeguards. **No study has been run yet. Everything below is planned.**

## The problem, in research terms
In the author's experience as a student in northern Bangladesh and as a tutor in Dhaka, mathematical "merit" tracks access more than ability. Access has four layers:

| Layer | Question | Can software help? |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Do good resources exist nearby? | **Yes.** A browser-based resource removes the distance. |
| Affordability | Can the family pay, and keep paying? | **Partly.** Free to use, but needs a device and data. |
| Know-how | Does the student know how to learn with the resource? | **Partly.** That is a design question this project studies. |
| Networks | Does the family know the system and its people? | **No.** Software cannot supply social capital. |

These layers draw on Bourdieu's distinction between economic, cultural and social capital, on the research literature on "shadow education" (paid private tutoring), and on critiques of meritocracy. Specific citations will be added in the literature review. The project's claim is deliberately narrow: it targets the first and third layers.

## Research questions
1. **Understanding:** Does direct manipulation with live values improve students' conceptual understanding of geometry, compared with textbook instruction on the same topic?
2. **Anxiety:** Does using Geometry Reader reduce maths anxiety, and does any reduction differ between students with high and low starting anxiety?
3. **Language:** Does pairing each formal term with a plain-language translation improve recall and correct use of the formal vocabulary?
4. **Access:** Is the tool usable on the low-cost Android phones and slow connections typical outside Dhaka?
5. **Equity:** Are any benefits larger, smaller or the same for students outside Dhaka compared with urban English-medium students?

## Hypotheses (untested)
- **H1:** Students using Geometry Reader show larger gains in conceptual understanding than a comparison group.
- **H2:** Maths anxiety falls after use, with larger reductions for students who start out more anxious.
- **H3:** Paired vocabulary improves correct use of formal terms, without reducing exam-style accuracy.
- **H4:** Watching a shape keep its rules while it is stretched (for example, a rectangle becoming a square) improves students' acceptance of inclusive class relationships, such as "every square is a rectangle", compared with fixed diagrams.

## Planned methods
- **Usability (first step):** think-aloud sessions with a small number of students, plus the System Usability Scale (SUS) questionnaire.
- **Anxiety:** a validated maths-anxiety scale (e.g. the Abbreviated Math Anxiety Scale, AMAS) before and after use. Adapting or validating it for Bangla will be part of the work.
- **Understanding:** pre- and post-tests of geometry concepts, mapped to the syllabus each group follows.
- **Design:** start with a within-class pre/post pilot; then a comparison-group study once the Bangla version exists.
- **Device testing:** page-load and frame-rate measurements on representative low-cost phones.

## Ethics and safeguards
- Participants are minors, so studies need **ethics approval** from a supervising institution, **parental consent** and **the student's own agreement**.
- No personal data is collected by the current software, and no tracking is built in. Adding any data collection requires a privacy review first.
- Students, schools and families will be anonymised in every publication and in this repository.
- Students who are neurodivergent or anxious may benefit most, and are also the most vulnerable. Participatory design with these students, their teachers and their families is preferred to designing on their behalf.

## What this project does not claim
- That Geometry Reader improves learning or reduces anxiety. Not yet measured.
- That it closes the access gap. At best it narrows one or two of the four layers.
- That the topic coverage maps one-to-one onto every syllabus. A lab-by-lab mapping to the NCTB textbooks is still to be done.
